# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Hiring decision-makers at game studios: design leads, creative directors, and
recruiters evaluating İlker Neslioğlu for a game designer role. They arrive
skeptical and time-poor, often on mobile, scanning many portfolios in a sitting.
Their job-to-be-done: decide quickly whether this person can think like a
designer (systems, players, craft) across multiple disciplines, and whether the
work shows real depth or surface polish. They reward evidence of process and
range; they punish filler.

## Product Purpose

A single-page portfolio that presents İlker as a thoughtful, multi-disciplinary
game designer through four projects of deliberately different shapes: a
hero hyper-casual title with hand-drawn illustration (Crazy Cups), a pixel-art
FPS rogue-like (MegaKill), and two documentation-led design works with no visuals
(Aye Captain!, Stick Sort). Success = a studio reviewer finishes the page
convinced of the designer's craft and thinking, and clicks the contact link.

The central challenge: each project has a different evidence type (tall
illustrations / pixel grids / pure text). The design must make the text-only
projects feel as substantial as the illustrated hero, never like afterthoughts.

## Brand Personality

Calm, precise, curious. The voice is reflective and honest about growth (a
career-switcher from consulting who taught himself Figma and pixel art), never
boastful. The interface should feel like quiet mastery: confident restraint,
intellectual curiosity, craft you notice on second look. Professional and calm
at rest, with moments of genuine innovation that reveal the passion underneath,
earned, never decorative.

## Anti-references

- **Generic dev-portfolio template.** No hero gradient, no skill bars, no
  identical project cards, no "Hi I'm X" typewriter intro.
- **Loud gamer aesthetic.** No neon-on-black RGB, no sci-fi HUD frames, no
  aggressive angular shapes, no esports energy.
- **Corporate SaaS landing.** No flat-blue startup look, no stock illustration,
  no feature-grid blandness.
- Overdesigned, cluttered, or hard-to-read layouts of any kind.

## Design Principles

1. **Every project earns equal weight.** Text-only work must feel as complete as
   the illustrated hero. The layout adapts to each project's evidence type
   instead of forcing one template.
2. **Innovation in service of story.** Motion exists to reveal how something
   works (cups stacking, pixels assembling, currencies flowing), never as
   decoration. One signature moment per section beats constant noise.
3. **Calm is the default, surprise is earned.** Restraint dominates; the wow
   moments land harder because the surrounding space is quiet.
4. **Mobile is the real product.** Studios review on phones. Every layout and
   animation is designed mobile-first and must work flawlessly there.
5. **Show the thinking, not just the result.** Foreground process, decisions,
   and what was learned, the evidence a designer reasons well.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG 2.1 AA: AA contrast across both the light and dark ends of the
scroll journey, full keyboard operability, visible focus states, semantic
landmarks. `prefers-reduced-motion` gets a full calm fallback: scroll-driven and
decorative animation collapses to instant or simple fade, with identical content
and no loss of meaning. Performance budget protects low-end mobile.
